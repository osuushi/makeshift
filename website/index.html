CAD For Everyone

You shouldn’t have to become a CAD expert to make the thing you have in mind. Makeshift is built around getting started, learning as you go, and letting coding agents handle the repetitive work.

Spend more time
making the thing.

A replacement part. A small enclosure. Something that only needs to fit on your desk. Getting an idea into a model should feel approachable, even if you’re new to CAD.

Start with a shape. Learn as you go.

Draw and drag to get started. Select something to see its controls, type a dimension when it needs to be exact, and discover tools as you need them. Simple starting points lead into the same tools you’ll use for more involved work.

Work on the part in front of you.

Makeshift works directly with solid geometry. Add material, cut it away, move a face, round an edge. You don’t have to plan a tree of features before you know what you’re making.

Sketches can have dimensions and constraints. Solids remain independent: editing an earlier sketch doesn’t automatically rebuild the part.

Give the repetitive work to an agent.

On desktop, a coding agent can inspect your model and use scripts to edit it. Let it handle repeated operations and calculations, then keep shaping the result by hand. Both work on the same geometry, and accepted modeling scripts can be undone.

Connect a separately installed coding agent. Its account and any service costs are separate from Makeshift.

Free as in beer. Free as in speech.

A tool you can
make your own.

Makeshift costs nothing to use. The source is available to study, change, and share under the LGPL. You can build it yourself, improve it, or take it in a different direction.

Your work saves to local files. Export STEP for solid geometry, or STL and 3MF for printing and other mesh workflows.
